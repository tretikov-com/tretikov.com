## Imported Claude Cowork project instructions

The era first. This is mid-2000s digital maximalism, the aesthetic that crystallized roughly between 2002 and 2007, centered on communities like deviantART, IDM and drum-and-bass album art, and the first generation of designers who had grown up using Illustrator rather than learning it as a second language. It looks confident and sometimes overwrought in equal measure, and that tension is structural.
Color. Every piece uses a palette of two to three chromatic decisions and then holds to them absolutely. The ground is either near-black or near-white. On top of that ground sits a single high-intensity chromatic accent. Neutrals do the middle work, sometimes as flat fills, sometimes rendered with just enough tonal variation to suggest three-dimensionality without committing to full shading. Nothing bleeds between zones. The color discipline is closer to a screen-printing sensibility than to painting.
Line. All edges are hard, all paths resolve to precise vector geometry. Where texture appears, it is made of more geometry: hatching built from parallelograms, not marks. The line weight varies meaningfully rather than randomly. Structural edges are heavy; interior articulation is thin, almost engraved. There is no blur, no drop shadow used as a crutch, no feathered gradient. Where glow or atmospheric depth exists it comes from layering flat translucent shapes, not from soft-edged filters. The result reads as technical, like documentation for something that does not exist.
Composition. Each image is organized around a radial or diagonal energy that departs from one zone of density and scatters toward open space, with the negative space functioning as deliberately as the marks. The empty areas are not accidents; they create the sense that the composition is still in motion. Elements do not fill the frame, they occupy it provisionally. There is always a focal mass, complex and layered, and then a long trail of diminishing fragments moving away from it. The trajectory matters: the eye enters through chaos and exits through sparseness.
Subject matter and the machine. The content clusters around mechanical and geometric forms: robots with biologically plausible articulation, wireframe polyhedra scattered as if by concussion, angular architectural shards exploding from a point source. Where organic creatures appear they are rendered in the same diagrammatic idiom as the machinery. Nothing is warm or soft. The machine and the creature are the same thing, subject to the same visual logic.
Typography. Where type appears it is condensed grotesque, almost always uppercase, set small relative to the overall composition. It reads as metadata rather than headline: spec sheets, catalog numbers, URLs. The type never competes with the image; it annotates it. Numeric hierarchies function the same way a part number functions on a technical drawing.
The specific texture that makes it feel right. An artist working in this aesthetic should think of each piece as the output of a fictional industrial process. The mechanical elements are detailed beyond functional necessity, as if the designer needed to convince the viewer the object could actually be built. The abstract elements carry the same quality: geometric forms feel like the aftermath of something structural collapsing or being assembled. The image implies a process, even when no process is depicted.
